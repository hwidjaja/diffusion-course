Why this course?

  • Attain unparalleled, grad school-level mastery — designed to bring you in the top 1% of experts in the topic
  • Delivered in my signature teaching style — grounded in intuition-building visuals and first-principles thinking, and loved by 10k+ researchers, engineers, and math and tech enthusiasts worldwide
  • Prepares you for the world of generative models more broadly — while building a differentiated expertise in its currently most successful subfield

The course's unique approach

  • Understand diffusion as performing gradient descent in image space (based on score matching) instead of the one-dimensional noising/denoising
  • Understand why diffusion shouldn't be possible, and the mathematical principles that made it finally happen
  • To show you that viewing diffusion in this way is much more interesting, practical, and teaches us a lot of things about other areas of machine learning

Back up visual intuition with grad school-level rigor

  • Graduate-level problem sets with full answer keys
  • All problems and solutions typed out in LaTeX, with my commentary
  • Video solutions where I walk you through the most challenging problems

Critically engage with frontier research

  • In Module 6, you'll use your newfound knowledge to critically engage with frontier research
  • Dissect a recent paper from a leading research group at Stanford
  • Understand why a "perfectly trained" diffusion model will only ever memorize the training data, and the theoretical conditions that prevent this in practice

Frequently asked questions

  • Who is this course for? AI/ML professionals, CS or engineering students, and researchers — if you already work with AI in your day-to-day, and you're interested in being among the best in generative models, you're a good fit.
  • Why this course over a college or grad class?
  • I'm completely new to generative models. Is this course for me? Yes! This course will best serve people with some AI or machine learning background, but assumes no specific prior knowledge in generative modeling.
  • What background do I need? You should be familiar with common machine learning concepts, like matrix multiplication, regression, and gradient descent. You'll find yourself most comfortable if you've taken at least one undergrad course on probability and linear algebra.
  • What does the course cover that the YouTube videos don't? This course provides theoretical rigor: highly technical lecture notes, problem sets, and solutions ensure mastery beyond what I can easily convey on YouTube.
  • I have a non-CS undergrad degree. Is this course for me? Yes — if you major in math, physics, or engineering, you'll likely be fine. Being a non-CS undergrad myself, I tried to make the course as inclusive as possible. The only "hard" requirement is having some prior exposure to machine learning.
  • Why are the exercises math-heavy instead of programming-heavy? The math in this course is designed to provide conceptual clarity, ultimately helping you decide what to implement in code. Programming-focused resources on diffusion models, of which there is an overabundance, often don't do a good job of communicating why certain implementation decisions are made.
